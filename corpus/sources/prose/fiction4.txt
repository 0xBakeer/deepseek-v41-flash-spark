The dispatcher's voice came through flat and unbothered, the way it always did, as though nothing
that happened on the water could be made more urgent by tone. "Vessel is taking water aft, four
persons on board, position two miles southwest of the point. Sea state four."

Ani had the boat off the trailer in eleven minutes, which was not a record but was respectable given
the tide. Her crew that night was Thom, who had done this for nineteen years, and a trainee named
Rekha whose entire experience of the sea was six weeks of drills in a harbour where the water never
did anything you did not expect.

Going out past the point the swell came from the side and the boat began the movement that people
either get used to in the first hour or never get used to at all. Rekha was quiet in a way that Ani
recognised, and Ani handed her the searchlight, because having a job is the only reliable cure.

They found the vessel by its sound before its light: a bilge pump running at the pitch a pump makes
when it is losing. It was an eight-metre cruiser with the stern sitting low and four people in life
jackets on the coachroof, and the moment the beam touched them one of them began to wave with both
arms, which is the thing everybody does and the thing that most often puts people in the water.

"Sit down," Thom said, not loudly, and somehow across thirty metres of wind the man sat down.

The transfer took nine minutes. Ani held the boat off the cruiser's quarter on the engines while Thom
worked, and Rekha did exactly what she had been told, in the order she had been told, and her hands
shook only afterwards, which is the correct time for them to shake.

On the way back the cruiser's owner kept trying to explain about the seacock, and Ani kept saying
that it did not matter now, and he kept explaining, because that is what people do when the thing has
stopped happening and they need somewhere to put it. Rekha sat beside him and listened to the whole
explanation twice. It occurred to Ani, watching her, that the crew list for next season had just
sorted itself out.
