"You're going to tell me it's fine," Nadia said.

"It's not fine. I'm going to tell you it's survivable, which is different." Peter set the folder down
without opening it. "How much do you want to know?"

"All of it."

"Then sit down, because the first part is boring and the boring part is the part that matters." He
turned the folder around. "We lost the contract in March. You knew that. What you didn't know is that
the contract was paying for the lease, and the lease has fourteen months to run, and the landlord is
not a sentimental man."

Nadia read the first page slowly. "Why didn't you say in March?"

"Because in March I thought I could replace it by June, and in June I thought I could replace it by
September, and each of those was a reasonable thing to think at the time." He shrugged, and it was
not a shrug that asked for anything. "That's not an excuse. It's just what happened."

"And now?"

"Now we have three options, and two of them are bad in ways you can see from here, so let me start
with the third." He took the second page out and laid it beside the first, squaring the edges the way
he did when he was nervous. "We sublet the back half. It's ugly, it means sharing the loading bay
with someone we haven't met, and it makes us look like a company that had to sublet the back half."

"Which we are."

"Which we are. And in fourteen months nobody will remember, if there is a company in fourteen months
to do the remembering."

She was quiet for a while. Outside, someone was reversing a van with the beeper going, over and over,
until it stopped.

"Do it," she said. "But I want to meet them before they sign. Not to approve. Just to meet."

"That's fair." He closed the folder. "For what it's worth, I should have come to you in March."

"Yes," she said. "You should have."
